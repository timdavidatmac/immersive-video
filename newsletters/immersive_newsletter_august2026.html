🎬 Apple Immersive Video

The Big Story

"Apple Immersive Video Is a Medium for a Physical Sense of Presence"

"With Apple Immersive Video, I can allow people to physically experience a real place, a person, and a moment — and turn a film they have watched into a memory of their own." — Carl Amadeus Hiller

With more than 20 years directing film and concert productions, Hiller has shifted his focus over the past year almost entirely to Apple Immersive Video, shooting on a Blackmagic URSA Cine Immersive. He recently published "One Year with Apple Immersive Video," a long-form essay he calls less a technical report than a personal account of real productions.

Hiller makes an observation early on: "Even a spectacular landscape or an exceptional building can eventually feel like a very high-quality spatial postcard. A location becomes a story only when I meet a person there, witness something happening, or build a relationship with someone and their activity." His answer is a formula he traces through his own work again and again: person, activity, place, and change. A view alone isn't a story. A person doing something specific, somewhere specific, changed by it, is. Hiller describes the first time Myerscough watched the completed version of "We Make Belonging": she was "almost shocked at first," describing the feeling as meeting her own digital twin.

There's technical content as well: he breaks down exactly why 90fps capture matters for how believable movement feels. He also raises whether Apple Immersive Video could work as a kind of coaching tool, letting someone see their own posture or presence from the outside. Hiller closes with a line worth repeating: "With Apple Immersive Video, I can allow people to physically experience a real place, a person, and a moment, and turn a film they have watched into a memory of their own."

Want to see his work for yourself in Vision Pro? Watch "Faces of Fourni", available now on Amplium.

Just Announced

"Friday Night Baseball" Comes to Apple Immersive

"For the first time, baseball fans can take in all the action of 'Friday Night Baseball' live in Apple Immersive on Apple Vision Pro, leveraging 3D video recorded in 8K with a 180-degree field of view. On August 28, viewers with Vision Pro will experience the iconic Red Sox vs. Yankees rivalry from unique camera angles that place viewers right in the game, immersed in the drama of each at-bat, the celebrations in the dugout after a big hit, close plays at the outfield wall, and more."

The broadcast features commentary from Paul Severino, Xavier Scruggs, and Michelle Margaux; dedicated replays; and an immersive graphics package with team lineups and stats floating in the viewer's space, all captured with ambisonic microphones for Spatial Audio during live breaks in the action.

One game each Friday will carry the Apple Immersive broadcast through September 18: Red Sox at Yankees on Aug 28, Angels at Pirates on Sept 4, Padres at Giants on Sept 11, and Giants at Dodgers on Sept 18, as Los Angeles looks to clinch a fifth consecutive division title.

Now in the Theater App

"The River You See" — Slow Down, Look Around, and Experience Hope

Produced by Sandwich Vision and SpatialGen, "The River You See" is a 4-minute Apple Immersive Video visit to Chattanooga's Tennessee Aquarium, where the film's own framing calls the conservation work happening there "equal parts joyful and marvelous." We flagged the 27-second teaser back in July; this is the full release, and it's live now in the Theater app.

The film puts you right inside the aquarium's habitats: ring-tailed lemurs perched on rocks and branches, tortoises gathered underfoot, the kind of close, unhurried encounter that's genuinely hard to get in person even with a ticket in hand. It's a good example of the format doing what it does best: less spectacle, more presence, and a real institution's actual conservation mission sitting right behind the visuals rather than as an afterthought.

Other Immersive Content

Mission Stratos — To the Edge of Space and Back

Mission Stratos follows a stratospheric balloon launched from Timmins Airbase, Canada, rising far above the clouds as part of the STRATOS program, a joint research effort between the Canadian and French Space Agencies. It was shot by The Dorothy Project on an Insta360 Titan (11K), so it's 360° content rather than Apple Immersive Video's stereoscopic spec, but the production quality is genuinely high-end, and the view speaks for itself.

What keeps this from being just a pretty view is what's actually behind it: real near-space research, built for STEM audiences, not a tourism reel. Mel Randall of Arluma360 described watching the raw footage for the first time as one of those moments that reminds you why this format exists at all: nothing else puts you at that altitude, looking down at the curve of the Earth, quite like this does.

From the Field — Productions & Creators

A.R. Rahman Brings Two Decades of Immersive Storytelling to Vision Pro

Two-time Oscar-winning composer A.R. Rahman ("Slumdog Millionaire," "127 Hours") has launched ARR Immersive on Apple Vision Pro, confirmed via a Variety exclusive. The launch library reworks footage from "Le Musk," his 360° film that debuted at the Cannes XR Festival, for Vision Pro's spatial format, alongside new content made exclusively for the platform: three Kathak dance performances merging Western orchestral scores with Indian classical dance, and "ARR Live," a dedicated section for his concert performances.

"With the ARR Immersive App, we wanted to create a space where audiences don't simply watch a performance or listen to a composition, they feel present within it. I believe this is an exciting new canvas for creators, and this is only the beginning." — A.R. Rahman

ZeusXR Player — One Link, Every Headset

ZeusXR, founded by Daniel Vega, has introduced a new distribution technology built around the simple idea of a single shareable link that adapts playback to the device that opens it. That means native MV-HEVC streaming on Apple Vision Pro, and interactive stereoscopic modes on Mac and mobile.

"Immersive distribution should not depend on the lowest common denominator." — Daniel Vega, Founder & CEO, ZeusXR

Two demos accompanied the launch — one of them uses footage from Hiller, our lead story above. Vega brings 25 years of experience in stereoscopic media to ZeusXR, and frames the player's anaglyph mode as "connecting the origins of stereoscopic imaging with the future we are now building."

News & Use Cases

Accessibility Is Now Part of USD's Core Schema

OpenUSD has formally added UsdUIAccessibilityAPI to its core schema, meaning accessibility metadata like labels, descriptions, and priority can now live directly inside a 3D asset, rather than being bolted on afterward. Blender 5.1 and Maya-USD already ship support, with accessibility data surfaced right in Maya's Attribute Editor.

Why this matters: Apple Vision Pro handles USDZ files natively, so accessibility metadata that travels with the asset, through Blender or Maya, straight into visionOS, closes a very important gap. For anyone building spatial experiences, accessibility can now be built into your files, not bolted on later.
